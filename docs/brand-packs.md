# Brand trouble-code packs

Generic codes (P0xxx, P2xxx, …) are explained by the obdex database. Manufacturer-specific codes (P1xxx, C1xxx, B1xxx, U1xxx, …) mean different things per brand, and sometimes per module, so Obdaryn explains them with **brand packs**.

## Where they live

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `tools/brand_packs/<brand>.py` | The source you edit. Families of codes are written once, in English and Romanian. |
| `src/core/data/dtc/brands/<brand>.json` | Generated by the script above and compiled into the app. Don't edit it by hand. |
| `tools/check_brand_pack.py` | The validator. Run it before every commit. |

```bash
python3 tools/brand_packs/hyundai_kia.py
python3 tools/check_brand_pack.py src/core/data/dtc/brands/hyundai-kia.json
```

The pack id must match the vehicle-profile id in `src/core/data/vehicles/` (for example `hyundai-kia`). The app picks the pack from the car's VIN.

## Rules

A wrong meaning on an airbag or ABS code misleads people about safety systems, so:

1. **Sources are required,** and each must cover that exact code.
   - References are `{"u": url, "o": true}`, where `"o"` flags an official manufacturer document. The app labels those links "official".
   - `"official"`: a Hyundai/Kia document (service bulletin, recall, or chart filed with NHTSA) states the meaning. Needs at least one official link.
   - `"two-sources"`: at least two independent public sources agree. Needs at least two links.
2. **Own words only.** Never paste service-manual text. The pack is GPL; manufacturer text is copyrighted.
3. **Module matters.**
   - Key an entry as `C1604@steering` when the same code means something else in another module.
   - Obdaryn never uses an entry written for a different module than the one that reported the code.
4. **Standard codes are left alone,** unless the brand adds real information, such as which clutch a solenoid letter drives. Such entries set `"override": true`.
5. **Leave it out when in doubt.** If sources disagree or the meaning depends on the year or engine, leave the code out and note why in the research notes below.

## Hyundai/Kia pack (2010–2016: Elantra MD/UD, i30 GD, Ceed JD, Cerato/Forte)

The pack covers 177 entries in English and Romanian, across:
- **ABS/ESC:** wheel-speed sensors, brake switch, yaw sensor, CAN, hydraulic unit;
- **power steering (MDPS);**
- **airbag:** every firing circuit, crash sensors, buckle switches, crash records;
- **TPMS,** body CAN, steering lock and automatic climate control;
- **engine:** knock-sensor bearing detection (P1326), the immobilizer family and the battery sensor;
- **6-speed automatic:** solenoid names.

Meanings were researched in November 2026, mainly from Hyundai/Kia documents filed with NHTSA (static.nhtsa.gov). Every entry lists its sources, and the app shows them as links.

**Left out on purpose**
- **Sources readable only through search-engine summaries:** P1610, P1674, P1675, P169A, C1213, and the TPMS sensor battery codes C1121–C1124.
- **Disputed or uncertain meaning:**
  - P1295, P1693, P1185 and P1186.
  - The B17xx pretensioner codes.
  - The B2510–B2517 lamp codes.
  - C1212, which Kia defines but Hyundai's wording is unconfirmed.
- **Kia-only meanings that would mislead other cars:** P2501–P2503, the battery-sensor meanings.
- **Models or years outside the scope:**
  - P1327, used only on 2019–2021 cars.
  - Parking-assist codes.
  - The 2016+ MDPS codes.

**Help wanted:** codes seen on your own Hyundai/Kia, with the scan-tool output and sources.
